# What the mod adds

[Back to the README](../README.md)

**Playing on a single screen** (emulators only)

- Menus normally displayed on the bottom screen are moved to the top screen, including the title screen and main menu, quest list, Canteen, yes/no prompts and menu descriptions.
- Shops, equipment sets, the Melding Pot, Palico menus, help screens, tutorials and the Arena are also displayed on the top screen, along with the Gathering Hall's menus, Guild Cards, friends, chat and gestures.
- Character creation also takes place on the top screen, and you can enter character names using the D-pad.
- In towns and Gathering Halls, X opens the World Map on the top screen, while L displays the town map.
- In a Gathering Hall, a box in the top-left corner displays each hunter's weapon, name, Hunter Rank and ticket.
- On the Guild Card, Y hides the menus surrounding the hunters so you can take pictures (press X while editing the card).
- The touchscreen remains fully functional whenever the bottom screen is visible.

**Locking on with L**

- Hold L and press Up on the D-pad to lock on to a large monster or switch between targets. The target monster's face is displayed on the top screen.
- You can turn it off on the **Options** page or in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings.

**Kick on Select**

- Select kicks, and only Start opens the menu.
- If you set the game's own **START** option to **Kick** (Options, page 2), they swap in quests: Start kicks, and Select opens and closes the menu.
- It belongs to the **Single screen** tab of the **Options** page: with that module off, Select works as in the original game (Layered Gear's color window still opens with SELECT).

**Skipping the death camera**

- When the camera shows a slain monster at the end of a quest, press Start to skip it.
- You can turn it off in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings.

**Skipping a monster's intro**

- Start does what the touch screen's Skip button does: it skips a monster's intro scene (Fatalis, Dalamadur and the others the game lets you skip).
- The scene's music stops with it, so the fight starts with its own music.
- Story scenes cannot be skipped, as in the original game.
- You can turn it off in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings.

**Skipping the prologue**

- A new character starts on arriving in Val Habar, without the prologue on the sand ship with Dah'ren Mohran.
- The character gets what the prologue gave: 1500z and the starting armor, worn.
- The Caravaneer's first talk, the Guild registration and the caravan tutorial stay as in the original game.
- Only characters created with the mod installed change; existing saves do not. You can turn it off on the **Options** page (Other / QoL tab).

**Holding A**

- Holding A with your weapon sheathed keeps picking up, gathering and carving, without pressing it again.
- In quest rewards and the Wyporium's Claim Items, A takes an item and moves to the next one, and holding it keeps going.
- You can turn it off on the **Options** page or in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings.

**Faster text**

- Hold B to speed through conversations.
- You can turn it off on the **Options** page or in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings.

**Faster camera**

- The camera can turn up to five times faster while you hold a button to turn it. The game's own speed is the default.
- Change it on the **Options** page or in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings.

**Better Camera**

- The C-Stick (New 3DS) turns the camera smoothly in any direction, diagonals too, faster the further you push it.
- It aims bowguns and the bow the same way, each at its own speed.
- The D-pad turns the camera as in the original game.
- In the game (START, third page, Mod Settings, Camera, Better Camera) you can turn it on or off, set **Max Speed** from x0.25 to x4 for the camera and the aims, and set **Acceleration** from 25% to 400%: how fast the turn reaches its speed and stops, relative to the speed.
- It is on by default, with Max Speed x1 and Acceleration 100 %. Camera Speed (D-pad) no longer changes it.

**Item wheel**

- In a quest, hold L and push the C-Stick towards one of eight directions to use the item set there. The camera stays still while L is held.
- It needs a C-Stick: a New 3DS or the emulator. An old 3DS cannot use it.
- Choose each direction's item and the wheel's place in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings, Radial Menu (Edit Slots and the menu's settings). The same row turns the wheel off.
- Trigger: Tilt uses the item as soon as the C-Stick points at it, Flick and Release when the stick comes back, and ZR when ZR is pressed while the C-Stick points at it. With ZR, the button does nothing else while L is held (no dash, guard or target switch).

**Damage numbers**

- Each of your hits shows its damage over the monster: gold on a weak spot, white elsewhere, grey when it bounces off. Poison is grey; blast and a mount's finish are gold.
- Three styles: Wilds, Rise and Classic, each with its own mark for critical hits.
- They are off by default. Turn them on in the **Options** page or in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings, Interface, Damage Numbers, which also has the style, the size, how long they stay and Show All Damage (every hit on the monster, not only yours). Felynes' damage never shows.

**Hunting Horn songs**

- With a Hunting Horn in a quest, the top screen lists its songs at its right edge, under the minimap: each song's notes,
  then its name.
- The songs that start with the notes you just played light up, and a song you just completed lights up brighter.
- A song whose effect you have (from your horn or another hunter's) is green until the effect runs out, and purple once
  that effect had its encore (the song played again while it was on); it blinks in the last five seconds. A weaker
  version of an effect you have stronger is coloured too; instant songs (healing, Sonic Waves) never are.
- In the game: START, third page, Mod Settings, Interface, Hunting Horn Songs. Show Horn Songs turns the list off (on by
  default); Highlight colours the song's name (Text, the default) or its band (Background).

**World-style sharpness gauge** (by Jamo, from the mod "MH4U MH4G World Style Sharpness Indicator", used with permission)

- In a quest with a blade, the sharpness bar shrinks as its colour wears down, as in Monster Hunter World, and fills
  again when you sharpen. Its colours are brighter and steady, and the gauge's frame is darker.
- It works with HD texture packs: the frame is the game's own picture, only darker.
- In the game: START, third page, Mod Settings, Interface, World Sharpness (on by default).

**Weapon tree**

- At the Smithy, Forge Weapons shows the weapon class you choose as a tree: every weapon you have discovered, joined
  to the ones it upgrades into. The game's stats and materials stay at the left.
- A weapon is discovered when the game's lists would show it, when it is an upgrade whose first material you have
  obtained, or when it lies between two discovered weapons, so a line is never cut in two.
- A on a weapon upgrades a copy from your box into it (the one you wear first) or forges it from scratch. When a
  weapon has both ways, a small window asks "Upgrade" or "Craft from scratch", and the materials at the left are
  always those of the choice under the cursor. The help line and the price question name the way in capitals.
- After every purchase, an upgrade too, the smith hammers it as in the game's forge and asks whether to equip it.
- Beside your money, a box like the money's says "Cost" and the price of the weapon under the cursor (to forge or to
  upgrade, as the materials at the left), grey while your money is short.
- A small hammer marks the weapons that can be forged from scratch. A greyed icon cannot be paid for yet, a gold
  plate is a weapon you own, E is the one you wear and a box one you keep in the box without wearing it.
- Each weapon shows its element or status icon at its upper right, the one the stats panel shows; grey when it needs
  Awaken.
- Choosing a weapon in Upgrade Equipment opens its tree with that weapon as the one upgraded. Bowguns and Insect
  Glaives keep the game's own screens there.
- X switches between stats, try on and comparison; L and R turn the stats' page, as in the game's forge; B goes back.
- It is on by default. In the game: START, third page, Mod Settings, Interface, Smithy View: Tree, or List for the
  game's own lists.

**Jukebox**

- Your Housekeeper has a new line, Jukebox, beside the Gallery. In it you make up to 8 playlists of up to 40 songs, named
  with the game's own keyboard.
- The songs are the game's themes: towns and halls, areas, monsters and more. A button plays the highlighted song
  while you edit a playlist.
- The active playlist plays in the towns instead of their own music, in order or shuffled. A separate option does
  the same in the Gathering Hall and Dundorma's Elder Hall.
- After each song's looped part has played a chosen number of times (1 to 9, 2 by default) it fades out (0 to 10 seconds,
  2 by default) and the next song follows.
- Playlists are saved with the game. "This session" turns the jukebox off when the game closes; "Always" keeps it on.
- It is on by default. Turn it off in the **Options** page, under Other / QoL.

**Layered Gear and the Wyporium**

- The Smithy has a new line, Layered Gear: each look of an armor, weapon or Felyne set is bought once with materials and money, and worn over what you wear without changing it. Armor looks show their own colors; on the armor screen, SELECT opens a free color window: choose the whole set or one piece, then a color. Relics (Guild Quest gear) have their own Relics line, below Felyne, priced like their monster's gear; looks you don't own show darker icons. A switch above the rank row (ZL, or up from the rank row, then A) lists only the looks you own.
- The Wyporium has a new line, Rare Materials Exchange: hand over common items of the monsters that drop a rare material until you have given enough, and it goes to your Item Box.
- With Monster Hunter XX's game (optional), Layered Gear also offers the looks of its weapons and armor, and the Wyporium trades the new rare materials those looks cost. Its Felyne costumes with no head piece (Happy, Navirou and others) show the costume's own head over any head look; a Felyne weapon that changes when drawn always shows its drawn form.
- In multiplayer, the other players with this mod see your Layered Gear and your Felyne's, and you see theirs. A player who lacks one of your looks (without Monster Hunter XX's game, say) or plays without the mod sees your real gear; everyone can still play together.
- Each character can hide the looks in the game: START, third page, Mod Settings, Interface, Layered Gear: No. Your hunter, your Felynes and the other players then show their real gear, and the others see yours. Everything bought, worn and dyed stays for Yes (the default), and the Smithy's Layered Gear line stays open. In town it changes at once; in a quest, at the next one.
- You can turn Layered Gear off on the **Options** page: you wear your real gear and see the other players' real gear. Your save keeps what you bought, wore and dyed, and the new materials in your Item Box, for when you turn it on again.

**A smaller HUD**

- The HUD elements displayed over the game (clock, health, stamina, sharpness, item selector, minimap, talk windows during quests, etc.) can be scaled down, moving towards their respective corners. Available sizes are 90%, 80%, 70% and 60%. The default size is 70%.

**Built-in cheats**

- Choose them on the **Options** page, and change them during play, for each character: START, third page, Mod Settings.
- 3D, glow and distant blur are off by default for a sharper image.
- Other available cheats are disabled by default: a fixed frame rate (every 5 from 30 to 60 FPS, emulator only), adjustable camera field of view, longer wait times when joining online rooms, and weapons' true attack in menus. The field of view, the online rooms fix and the true attack work on a real 3DS too.
- **⚠️ EXPERIMENTAL, expect problems:** turn on **Allow other aspect ratios** and the game and its menus can fit screens from 4:3 to 32:9. It is disabled by default, and menus, prompts or backgrounds may be out of place or stretched. Your emulator must show the picture in that shape (in Azahar: Single Screen with Stretch). You can also change it during play: START, third page, Mod Settings. Please report any problem you see.
